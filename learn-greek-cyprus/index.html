Learn Greek in Cyprus: what to learn first and how to practise

For expats in Limassol, Nicosia, Larnaca and Paphos: which Greek to learn, how the Cypriot dialect fits in, where to find classes, and how to turn daily life into practice.

Updated September 30, 2026 · WCBO LLC

Short answer

Learn Standard Modern Greek first: it is the language of schools, official letters and the news, and everyone in Cyprus understands it. Take classes if you like structure, practise 15 to 20 minutes a day, and use Greek in shops, cafés and with neighbours. Cypriot words and pronunciation will come with time.

Why learn Greek if you live in Cyprus

English is widely spoken, especially in Limassol, so it is possible to live in Cyprus for years without Greek. People who learn it usually do so for practical reasons:

  • Paperwork. Letters from the tax department, utility bills, municipality notices and school messages often arrive in Greek.
  • Everyday life. Neighbours, the περίπτερο (kiosk), the local bakery, the plumber and older Cypriots appreciate even a few words, and conversations change when you start in Greek.
  • Children. Kids in Greek-speaking schools learn fast; parents want to keep up with homework and teachers.
  • Work. Many jobs in the public sector, healthcare, retail and customer service need Greek.
  • Residence and citizenship. Some long-term residence and naturalisation routes ask for a Greek language certificate. More on that below.

Standard Greek vs Cypriot Greek

Greek is the official language of the Republic of Cyprus together with Turkish. In practice there are two layers:

Standard Modern GreekCypriot Greek
WhereSchools, universities, news, official documents, forms, signsHome, friends, the market, the village, much of everyday speech
Written?Yes, the norm for all writingMostly spoken; written informally in messages and social media
SoundAs taught in textbooks and appsSome consonants sound different, for example και (and) often sounds like “tše” and is written τζιαι
Wordsτι; (what?)ίντα; (what?), plus other local words and endings
For learnersStart hereAbsorb it gradually by listening to people around you

Cypriots switch to standard Greek easily when they hear a learner, so you will be understood from day one. Understanding fast Cypriot conversation is a later stage, and it comes from exposure, not from a separate course.

Where to learn Greek in Cyprus

  • State Adult Education Centres (Επιμορφωτικά Κέντρα) run by the Ministry of Education, Sport and Youth offer Greek for adults in towns and villages across the island. Registration usually opens before the autumn term.
  • The School of Modern Greek at the University of Cyprus in Nicosia runs structured Greek courses for foreigners.
  • Private language schools in Limassol, Nicosia, Larnaca and Paphos offer group courses, intensives and exam preparation.
  • Private tutors, in person or online, for flexible schedules and speaking practice.
  • An app for daily practice between classes. Classes give you structure; daily practice is what makes it stick.

Schedules, fees and locations change every year, so check directly with each provider before you plan around them.

Turn Cypriot daily life into practice

  1. Start every interaction in Greek. Καλημέρα at the bakery, ευχαριστώ at the till. See our basic Greek phrases.
  2. Read what arrives in your mailbox. Bills and notices are short, repetitive and full of useful words.
  3. Practise the situations you actually meet. The landlord, the pharmacy, the kiosk, the municipality office. In Greekly you tell the app where you live and what you do, and the dialogues and letters are written around that.
  4. Listen to Cypriot radio and TV in the background. Your ear adjusts to the local sound long before you can reproduce it.
  5. Find one conversation a week: a tutor, a colleague, a neighbour, or a language exchange.

Greek for residence and citizenship in Cyprus

Cyprus links some residence and citizenship routes to a certified level of Greek. As of 2026, legal guides describe the standard naturalisation route as requiring Greek at B1, while A2 appears in long-term residence rules and in some faster routes. The Ministry of Education's Examination Service organises the Greek language certification exams in Cyprus (the Certificate of Attainment in Greek, set by the Centre for the Greek Language) every May, and a separate exam on the contemporary political and social reality of Cyprus for third-country nationals.

Requirements change and depend on your situation, so always confirm the current rules with the Civil Registry and Migration Department or a qualified lawyer. For how the exam works and how to prepare, see Greek A2 exam and What the A2 level of Greek means.

Frequently asked questions

Should I learn Cypriot Greek or Standard Greek in Cyprus?

Start with Standard Modern Greek. It is used in schools, news, official documents and forms, and every Cypriot understands it. Once you are comfortable, you will pick up Cypriot pronunciation and words naturally from people around you.

Is Cypriot Greek a different language?

No. Cypriot Greek is a dialect of Greek. It has its own pronunciation, some different words and endings, and it is what most people speak at home and with friends, but speakers of Standard Greek and Cypriot Greek understand each other.

Can I live in Cyprus without Greek?

Many people do, especially in Limassol, where English is widely spoken. But Greek makes daily life easier, from letters and bills to neighbours, and some residence and citizenship routes ask for a Greek language certificate.

Where can I take Greek classes in Cyprus?

Options include the state Adult Education Centres (Epimorfotika Kentra) run by the Ministry of Education, the School of Modern Greek at the University of Cyprus, private language schools in every city, and private tutors, in person or online.

Do I need a Greek certificate to live in Cyprus?

For short stays and most work permits, no. Long-term residence and naturalisation routes can require proof of Greek at a set level. The rules change, so check the current requirements with the Civil Registry and Migration Department. See our page on the Greek A2 exam.
